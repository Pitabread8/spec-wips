His top donors, according to campaign finance records, include Transport Workers Union Local 100, a union representing over 40,000 transit workers in New York City; GP Construction, a construction company; and Voices of Teachers for Education, a PAC representing public school and higher education educators across New York state.

Donors’ interests overlap with several areas of Wright’s legislative work. Wright serves on the assembly’s transportation committee and five other standing committees, including those on economic development and banks. He has sponsored Bill A04876, which would introduce an interdisciplinary climate change curriculum in public schools, and has pushed for legislation on incarceration, education, healthcare, and affordable housing.

Some of those connections predate Wright’s own assembly career. His father, former Assembly member Keith Wright, chaired the state assembly’s housing committee before his current position as the chair of the Manhattan Democratic Party. Keith Wright has partnered in the past with Genesis Companies, the parent company of one of Wright’s top donors, GP Construction, on affordable housing projects in Harlem.

Wright has also made affordable housing a campaign priority. In June, he joined Mamdani at the ribbon-cutting for ANCP Morningside, a project that converted three formerly abandoned buildings into 36 permanently affordable co-op homes. The initiative also tapped Genesis Companies for the city’s Block by Block housing plan, a sweeping agenda launched by Mamdani’s administration to build 200,000 new affordable homes and preserve 200,000 more.

The incumbent received PAC donations from 47 committees—more than Avila Chevalier and Northrup. Six of those PACs, including Home Healthcare Workers of America PAC, were based outside New York state.

Wright’s top PAC donors include groups that emphasize fighting division and extremism , such as Stand Up PAC, which promotes a politics of “hope and commonsense,” according to its website.
“We identify leaders dedicated to fighting extremism, protecting our right to vote, and defending our democracy,” its mission statement reads.

Other major PAC donors include the Speaker Heastie PAC, associated with Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie, and Solidarity PAC.

Solidarity PAC’s top donors have funneled money to support Wright’s state assembly race. In May, Adeena Rosen, the chair of Solidarity PAC and a Scarsdale, New York, resident with no known affiliation to Harlem, contributed $25,000 to Moving Harlem Forward, a super PAC formed to boost Wright in his primary against Blackburn, according to state campaign finance records. During the same period, Rosen donated $3,000 directly to Wright’s campaign.

Blackburn told Spectator in April that he suspected the super PAC could be helping with legal fees for the challenge against his signatures.

Wright’s policy platform does not refer to Israel and focuses instead on his position as a state lawmaker.

The assemblymember’s team did not respond to a request for comment on the role Moving Harlem Forward played in Wright’s campaign or his position on Israel.
