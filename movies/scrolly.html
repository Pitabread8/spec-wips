When it comes to cinema, Columbia’s got taste! The largest genre ranked by students is Arthouse, defined by independent, experimental, and director-driven films. The genre has 36 entries and 80 weighted points.

Second-highest in weighted points is the Drama category, with 79 points across 29 ranked films, and Whiplash and Aftersun being the favorites. The latter received a comment calling it “the greatest portrayal of both nostalgia and depression.” The contributor cited Aftersun’s emotional climatic crash, extraordinary score, and actors Paul Mescal and Frankie Corio as what make it a “timeless masterpiece.” For another commenter, the film resonated because of its resemblance to their own dynamic with their father. Connections like these might help explain why this category appealed to students.

The Comedy and Romance category comes third with 76 weighted points. La La Land and Pride and Prejudice lead the total 28 films in points. One student appreciated Pride and Prejudice’s “ability to capture the class war” alongside romance and another wrote that La La Land was their proof that “love is possible.” While these films fall within the most lighthearted category, their themes still struck an emotional chord.

While the Sci-Fi and Fantasy genre sees fewer nominations at just 19 films, its 60 weighted points are carried by films ranging from Everything Everywhere All at Once, Interstellar, and The Truman Show. One student called Interstellar “a masterpiece in film, plot, acting, thrill, everything,” while another described The Truman Show as belonging to “my favorite genre I cannot name.” Everything Everywhere All at Once was praised for “Queer Asian representation [and] finally seeing people who look like you on the big screen.” These films depict realities unlike ours, yet still reflect what is essential about the world we live in.

Rian Johnson's Knives Out narrowly wins in Crime and Action. The genre falls in the middle with 58 weighted points across 25 films. The most extensive review for this category describes The Hateful Eight as a “great cozy historical film” with “really interesting reflections on post-Civil War era feelings.”

Scores for the Animated and Family category are modest overall, with Princess Mononoke and How to Train Your Dragon standing out prominently. Commenters said these picks were formative: their animated medium moved them before they even had the language to explain why. A contributor wrote that Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind the show had them crying as they watched from their parents bed because it tackled humanity’s most profound issues through non-human characters, turning heavy truths into something “digestable.” For Princess Mononoke, someone wrote a similar sentiment, “it instilled me with morals.”

Rounding out the list is the World Mainstream category, which comprises international films created outside of Hollywood. Though it’s the smallest pool, with only 11 movies and 34 total points, its individual titles perform incredibly well. Mexican film Y tu mamá también is a standout, ranking second out of the entire survey pool with comments like “beautiful” and “amazing plot twist.” Additionally, the aforementioned Princess Mononoke, a Japanese production, ranks fifth for this category.
