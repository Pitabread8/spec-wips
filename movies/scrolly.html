When it comes to cinema, Columbia’s got taste! The largest genre ranked by students was arthouse, defined by independent, experimental, and director-driven films. The genre has 36 entries and 80 weighted points.

Second-highest in weighted points was the drama category, with 79 points across 29 ranked films. “Whiplash” and “Aftersun” were the favorites. The latter received a comment calling it “the greatest portrayal of both nostalgia and depression.” The contributor cited the film’s emotional climatic crash, extraordinary score, and actors Paul Mescal and Frankie Corio as what makes it a “timeless masterpiece.” For another commenter, the film resonated because of its resemblance to their own dynamic with their father. Connections like these might help explain why this category appealed to students.

The comedy and romance category came in third with 76 weighted points. “La La Land” and “Pride and Prejudice” were on top, out of 28 films in total. One student appreciated how “Pride and Prejudice” has an ability “to capture the class war” alongside romance, and another wrote that “La La Land” was “so heartbreaking and meaningful and it really portrays love.” While these films fall within the most lighthearted category, their themes still struck an emotional chord.

While the sci-fi and fantasy genre saw fewer nominations at just 19 films, its 60 weighted points are carried by films ranging from “Everything Everywhere All at Once,” “Interstellar,” and “The Truman Show.” One student called Interstellar “a masterpiece in film, plot, acting, thrill, everything,” while another described “The Truman Show” as belonging to “my favorite genre I cannot even give a name to.” On the other hand, “Everything Everywhere All at Once” was praised for “Queer Asian representation, finally seeing people who look like you on the big screen.” These films depict realities unlike ours, yet still reflect what is essential about the world we live in.

Rian Johnson’s “Knives Out” narrowly won in the crime and action category. The genre falls in the middle, with 58 weighted points across 25 films. The most extensive review for this category describes “The Hateful Eight” as a “great cozy historical film” with “really interesting reflections on post-Civil War era feelings.”

Scores for the animated and family category are modest overall, with “Princess Mononoke” and “How to Train Your Dragon” standing out prominently. Commenters said these picks were formative: Their animated medium moved them before they even had the language to explain why. One contributor wrote that “Princess Mononoke” had “instilled me with morals” and another declared it a “peak movie.”

Rounding out the list was the World Mainstream category, which comprises international films created outside of Hollywood. Though it was the smallest pool, with only 11 movies and 34 total points, its individual titles perform incredibly well. Mexican film “Y tu mamá también” is a standout, ranking second out of the entire survey pool with comments like “beautiful” and “amazing plot twist.” Additionally, the aforementioned “Princess Mononoke,” a Japanese production, ranks fifth for this category.
